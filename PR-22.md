The user's model:

> "only left and right edging, and account for a exact ish pixel amount of the left and right of a cell and extrapolate the rest from there"

Pairing every border individually meant **every internal border had to be read correctly or the row was wrong**, and each failure so far has been one border missed, doubled or a pixel out. A row's cells are identical and evenly spaced, so a row only has three numbers in it: where it starts, how wide a cell is, and the pitch. Each is now a median over the whole row — a bad border becomes one bad vote instead of one bad cell boundary.

## Two things the fixtures forced, both of which broke on the first attempt

- **Rebuilding every cell from the lattice moved the good ones** by a pixel or two, which was enough to lose a plate crop on one cell and lift a plain cell's gold ring into the ambiguous band. Only cells that *disagree* with the lattice are rebuilt; a cell already on it keeps the measurement taken from its own borders.

- **Judging that agreement by the left edge rejected correct cells.** A decorated cell — the gilded gold frame, or the blue border the first cell in a row often carries — is wider than its slot and overflows it on both sides, so its left edge is legitimately early while its centre is exactly on the slot. Agreement is judged on centres.

## Result

`4 Raw.png` row 0 — the row clipped by the top of the capture region, and the last thing still visibly wrong on the user's capture — had cells 60, 47 and 39 wide against a pitch of ~54. It is now eight regular cells with both gilded runes found at ring 0.293.

All four fixtures pass unchanged otherwise: 4 to 10 rows, 2 to 8 icons per row.

## Also

The carried marker was mid grey — the one colour a parchment panel of brown ink and gold frames gives no contrast against, so it read as a shadow rather than a mark. It is now the same red as the cross style: it carries the meaning on its own and is the furthest hue from the green and orange used for runes still worth taking.
